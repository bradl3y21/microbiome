# Calibration of the realism layer against published data

This file is generated by `python -m microbiome.calibration report` from `data/calibration_targets.json` and `data/calibration_fit.json`; `tests/test_calibration.py` fails if it is out of date.

## What this does and does not show

Every number the simulation produces for growth, uptake, swimming and chemotaxis was first a guess. Here each is set against a measurement from the literature, read from the cited text (the targets file keeps the evidence and the source of each). Two kinds of result must not be confused:

* **Matched by construction.** A free parameter was solved so the simulation reproduces the number. Agreement is guaranteed and says nothing about whether the model is right.
* **Held out.** The number was not used in any fit. Agreement here is the only evidence the model's structure, rather than its tuning, is sound, and it is weak: there are few such checks and they are consistency checks, not independent experiments.

Nothing was compared with data for a *population* (a growth curve, a colony, a competition) and nothing here validates the ecology. Four organisms are fitted to batch growth on glucose with air (M. xanthus, which does not use sugar, only to its gliding speed, reversals and cell length).

## Not calibrated at all

These are still the original, unmeasured guesses (each is a named parameter in `docs/REALISM.md`): the lag-phase time constants; starvation death rate and time constant; sporulation commitment thresholds (only the 8 h duration is checked); germination rate; quorum-signal production and threshold; biofilm matrix time constants and its protection factors; toxin production and potency; acid damage; pH buffering; the receptor adaptation time (2 s) and sensing constant; collision stiffness and cohesion; transformation rates; the Michaelis constants of oxygen and acetate and every constant for lactose; the B. subtilis glucose Ks; B. subtilis cell mass. Rod width and wet volume per gram of dry mass (3.3 um^3/pg, against 3.1 derived from Neidhardt 1996 with an assumed density) are plausible, not fitted.

## Known mismatches

* **Acetate overflow depends on sugar level, not growth rate.** Basan et al. 2015 find none below 0.76/h in batch culture (K-12 NCM3722 in MOPS medium); the oxygen uptake used for the E. coli fit (strain C-3000, M9) implies about 21 % of glucose carbon leaves as acetate at 0.65/h. The two data sets disagree by strain and medium, and the model follows the one it was fitted to. It does reproduce Kayser et al.'s observation that glucose-limited chemostat growth below D = 0.3/h makes no acetate.
* **Tumbles take no time.** Cells are about 30 % faster on average than the same cells would look in tracking (0.19 s of every 0.83 s is a tumble).
* **Oxygen diffuses 30 % too slowly** (1400 against 2000 um^2/s) because the explicit scheme is unstable above about 1500 at this step; acetate is capped likewise.
* **All swimmers share every sugar Ks and the chemotactic gain**; each has its own swimming speed, and P. aeruginosa its own run time and reversal angle. Chemotaxis was calibrated on E. coli alone.
* **Maintenance of P. aeruginosa, S. aureus and M. xanthus is E. coli's assumed value**, a placeholder (no coefficient was found).
* **Biomass is glucose-equivalent in the model** (40 % carbon by mass, against about 48 % for real dry matter), so carbon fractions derived from the model, such as the 21 % of glucose carbon that the E. coli fit sends to acetate, are internally consistent but only approximate; growth, uptake and dry mass are matched in their own units.
* **Cell size does not vary with growth rate** (the Schaechter growth law is not modelled): the division mass is fixed at the value for 0.65/h.

## Reproducing

```
python -m microbiome.calibration fit      # solves the parameters and rewrites data/calibration_fit.json
python -m microbiome.calibration check    # measures every target against the committed fit (exit 1 on a failure)
python -m microbiome.calibration cycle    # the whole-cell-cycle check above (several minutes)
python -m microbiome.calibration report   # regenerates this file
```
